The plane's still taxiing and the person next to you has already booked a ride. You pull out your phone. Full signal bars, but the page just spins. That usually isn't Saily being broken. It's a switch in the wrong position.

<mark><strong>When you land, turn data roaming on for the Saily line and off for your home SIM. One on, one off, and you're online without anything extra on your bill.</strong></mark>

[Beginner lesson 3](/en/start/3-land/) walks through switching things on after you land. This step covers why it works that way, how to switch between lines, and what to check, in order, when there's no internet or it's slow.

## Activation happens by itself when you arrive

Plenty of people land and look for an "activate" button in the app. You don't need one. If you installed the eSIM before you left and turned on its data roaming, the plan activates on its own when you reach your destination and connect to a supported local network ([Help Center](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/15751572038300)). What you do is make sure these are set right:

- The Saily line is switched on in your phone's settings.
- Its data roaming is on.
- Data roaming is off for your home SIM. If your phone doesn't support dual SIM, switch your home SIM off entirely and keep only Saily on.
- On Android, also check the <Term k="APN" />. If a Saily APN is there, you're fine; if not, open the Saily app and keep it open for up to 5 minutes, and it sets the APN up for you.

See [How to check if my Saily eSIM is activated?](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/12823108536476)

<Callout type="ok">
Turn off Wi-Fi and open a web page. If it loads, Saily is working.
</Callout>

Once it's active, remember that <u>a plan can't be paused after it's activated</u>. The validity period keeps running whether you use the data or not.

## Why one needs roaming on and the other off

<Term k="漫游" /> just means your phone is outside its own carrier's area, borrowing someone else's network. A Saily eSIM always borrows a local partner network, so abroad it's always roaming, and <u>without data roaming on, it can't connect</u>. Saily doesn't charge extra for that roaming ([Help Center](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/12838788763164)).

Your home SIM is the opposite. When it borrows a network abroad, your home carrier charges roaming, so you want it to stay away from data. The Help Center lists several ways to keep it out, from the most thorough to the lightest:

- Take your physical SIM out before you travel.
- Turn on airplane mode before you leave home coverage, then turn on only Saily when you arrive.
- Switch the home line off completely. On iPhone, tap your home SIM under **Settings → Mobile Service** and turn off **Turn On This Line**; on Samsung, switch it off under **Settings → Connections → SIM manager**.
- At the very least, turn off automatic data switching. On iPhone it's **Allow Mobile Data Switching** under **Mobile Data**; on Samsung it's **Data switching**.

The last one is the easiest to miss. Data switching means that when Saily's signal dips, your phone quietly falls back to your home SIM for data, and that's home roaming data. When you get back, check your usage in the Saily app and your home carrier's bill for anything unexpected. See [How to avoid roaming charges while using Saily?](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/20793044376092)

If you want your home number for calls and verification texts, don't switch the whole line off. Turn off only its data roaming. Taking calls may also count as roaming, so ask your carrier first ([Help Center](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/16747226859164)). For running both numbers, see the how-to [Keep your home number alongside Saily](/en/howto/dual-sim/).

## Switching between lines

With two lines, calls and data can each use a different one. Your phone asked you during the install, and the Help Center suggests your home SIM as the default line and Saily for mobile data ([Help Center](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/15751399206172)). To change it later, go back to **Mobile Data** (on Android, **Mobile data**) and pick the other line.

If you kept using your home data until you left, this is where you switch on landing. Turn on Saily's data roaming first, then change mobile data from your home SIM to Saily. When you get home, do the reverse and switch mobile data back to your home SIM. If you won't use the Saily line for a while, you can switch it off. On iPhone, turn off its **Turn On This Line**; on Android, switch it off in **SIM manager** ([Help Center](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/16436358702108)). Switching off isn't deleting, so you can turn it back on for your next trip.

## Signal but no internet: check in this order

When you've got bars but pages won't load, the Help Center says it's usually that Saily isn't selected for mobile data, data roaming is off, or the APN is wrong. The order below follows the Help Center's troubleshooting guide ([Help Center](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/12838788763164)). After each change, turn off Wi-Fi and try a web page, because with Wi-Fi on you can't tell whether Saily is working.

1. Are you in a country your plan covers? A plan only connects where it applies, and with the wrong country, no setting will help.
2. Does the plan have data left? Check what remains in the Saily app.
3. Are the three switches right? The Saily line is on, it's selected for mobile data with data switching off, and its data roaming is on.
4. Turn airplane mode on, wait 15 seconds, and turn it off, so your phone reconnects to the local network. Give it a minute, then try again.
5. Set network selection to automatic. If you're near a border and your phone has latched onto a neighbouring country's network, turn automatic off and choose a local network by hand.
6. Is the APN `wbdata`?
7. Restart your phone.
8. Switch the Saily line from 5G to 4G/LTE. Some local networks don't have 5G everywhere.
9. Reset network settings. This restores Wi-Fi passwords, data roaming and the APN to factory defaults, so afterwards turn Saily's data roaming back on, select it for data, and check the APN again.

If none of that works, the last resort is deleting the eSIM in your phone's settings, restarting, and installing it again from the app. <u>Don't do this in a country where Saily is restricted</u>, because you may not be able to download it again, and never use **Delete eSIM** in the app.

The Help Center has separate articles for where each setting lives on [iPhone](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/13357933521436), [Android](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/13338388172828) and [Google Pixel](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/13667167229596). For no signal at all, see the how-to [No signal, or signal but no internet](/en/howto/no-signal/).

## Slow? First check your high-speed data

When things are slow, don't start changing settings. Open the Saily app and see how much high-speed data you have left. Once it's used up, speed is capped at up to 1 Mbps, and changing settings, restarting or contacting support won't make it faster. You have to wait for the reset. Unlimited plans usually reset at local midnight, and Ultra at 00:00 UTC at the start of the next billing cycle. A regular plan that's used up simply has no data left, so add data or buy a new plan ([Help Center](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/12838788763164)).

If you still have high-speed data and it's slow, the Help Center suggests these: turn airplane mode on and off, restart your phone, turn off automatic network selection and pick a local operator by hand (ask support if you're not sure which), switch from 5G to 4G/LTE, and check the APN is `wbdata`. Coverage also drops indoors, underground, in thick-walled buildings and in remote areas, so step near a window or outside and test again. For each system's steps, see the Help Center's articles for [iPhone](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/15492404637084), [Android](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/15494022819228) and [Google Pixel](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/15636670187164).

Before contacting support, have these ready to save a lot of back and forth: your phone model, which country you're in, which steps you've tried, and a screenshot of your <Term k="EID" />. For slow speeds, support may also ask you to run a test at [fast.com](https://fast.com/), tap **Show more info**, and send your download speed, upload speed and latency. You'll find support under **Help** in the Saily app, by tapping **Chat with us**, 24/7.

## Going to Turkey? Prepare a few extra things

Turkey is a supported destination, covered by country plans, the Europe and Middle East and North Africa regional plans, the Global plan, Ultra and Unlimited plans, and Saily voice services work there too. The trouble is elsewhere. Inside Turkey, the Saily app and website may not load, and top-ups aren't available. So the Help Center is clear that you should buy, install and activate your plan before you enter Turkey ([Help Center](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/29110860512028)).

Before you go, do three more things:

- Set up top-up. The Help Center suggests turning it on before you travel, so you can still add data if the app is unreachable. [Step 4](/en/guide/4-manage/) shows how.
- Know where to see your usage without the app. On iPhone, scroll down in **Settings → Mobile Service** to see how much the Saily line has used; on Android, tap the Saily eSIM under **Settings → Connections** (or **Network & internet**).
- Don't delete or reinstall the eSIM while you're in Turkey.

If you arrive and realise you never installed it, look in the email inbox registered to your Saily account for a message titled "Hey, it's time to set up your eSIM!" and follow its manual install instructions. If you can't find that email, contact support.

If you're there and can't get online, first work out whether nothing loads at all, or only the Saily app and website won't open. The second isn't an eSIM problem. If nothing loads, confirm the eSIM was installed before you arrived. In your settings it may be called Business, Mobile Data, Secondary, Personal, Travel, Saily or Webbing. Then confirm it's on and its data roaming is on. And if a refund link won't open while you're in Turkey, that's the local restrictions too, so ask support to handle it.

In Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and mainland China, the Saily app or website may also be blocked, and the advice is the same. Install before you go, and don't delete it while you're there ([Help Center](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/12838788763164#travel)).

You can now get online abroad with Saily, and your home SIM won't quietly run up data. Next, see how much data you have left and how to add more.

## Stuck?

<Q q="I landed a while ago and still have no signal.">
<p>Check you're in a country your plan covers and the Saily line is on, then turn airplane mode on and off. Near a border, your phone may have joined a neighbouring country's network, so turn off automatic network selection and choose a local operator. For more, see the how-to <a href="/en/howto/no-signal/">No signal, or signal but no internet</a>.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="I can't find an activate button in the Saily app.">
<p>There isn't one. Once it's installed with data roaming on, the plan activates by itself when you arrive and connect to a local network.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="My home bill had a roaming charge after the trip.">
<p>Most likely your home SIM used data abroad, usually because its data roaming was on or automatic data switching wasn't off. Ask your home carrier what the charge was for, and next time turn both of those off before you go.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="My phone's data count doesn't match the Saily app.">
<p>That's normal. Your phone's counter may have started on a different date and may include other SIMs and Wi-Fi. Trust the Saily app; see <a href="https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/31092295169948">How Saily data plans work</a>.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="My Pixel's eSIM stops working after a restart, and other providers' eSIMs won't install either.">
<p>The Help Center says the built-in eSIM chip can fail on some Pixels running Android 16 or 17 (mostly the Pixel 9 Pro, 9 Pro XL, 10 Pro, 10 Pro XL and 6a), and only a repair or replacement fixes it. Contact Google Support about the phone, and Saily support about your plan.</p>
</Q>
