A lot of people buy the Saily US number for one reason: when an app outside their country asks for a phone number, they finally have one that can get the code. Then the moment comes to type it in, and the doubts start. Will WhatsApp take it? Will PayPal? Will ChatGPT just refuse? By the end of this page you'll know which apps have Saily's own list behind them, which ones other users have only reported working, and which ones you shouldn't count on. You'll also know what each app on the list is actually for.

Here's the short version. <mark>Apps on the official list are the safest bet. Most chat, social and account sign-ups have worked for other users too. Banks and payment apps are the pickiest, and AI services are the least predictable. Whatever the app, test the number once before you link an account that matters.</mark>

## Why not every app takes it

It's a real US number, assigned through the North American Numbering Plan, and it's yours alone. But the carrier behind it runs on internet-based (VoIP) technology, so apps that check number types will label it "VoIP" rather than "mobile." Most services don't care and send the code anyway. The ones that do care are mostly certain banks, financial and payment apps, and platforms with strict security rules. That's their policy, and Saily can't change it ([official article](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/27948689277596)).

So whether it works is never really up to Saily. It's up to the app you're signing up for, and apps change their rules whenever they like. That's why every list below is a guide, not a promise.

## The official list: commonly reported as working

Saily's help center keeps two lists, and says plainly that neither is complete or guaranteed and both can change ([the lists](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/27948689277596#h_01KTBC4HD8WJVE4A7NA0M88M8P)). The difference is when the code gets sent. A one-time password (OTP) is the code you get once when you sign up or log in. Two-factor authentication (2FA) means you link the number to your account, and from then on every login asks for your password plus a code sent to the number.

**Commonly reported as working for one-time codes:**

- **WhatsApp**: the world's most used chat app, and you need a phone number to sign up. It only checks the number once, at sign-up. After that it runs on any internet connection, whether or not that number is in your phone ([official article](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/13718527919900)).
- **Signal**: a chat app built around encryption, also tied to a phone number.
- **Viber**: chat and internet calls, popular in Eastern Europe and the Middle East, and it signs you up with a phone number too.
- **Airbnb**: for booking homes and short-term rentals around the world. It asks you to verify a phone number when you sign up and book.

**Commonly reported as working for 2FA:**

- **PayPal**: an online wallet for paying and getting paid, common for shopping abroad and for being paid by people in other countries.
- **Wise**: international transfers and a multi-currency account, for exchanging money and sending it abroad.
- **Venmo**: how people in the US send each other money and split bills.
- **Cash App**: another US money-transfer app, which can also buy stocks.
- **Chime**: a US online bank with no branches.
- **Bunq**: a Dutch online bank, for opening an account in Europe.
- **Revolut**: a multi-currency account and card, handy for spending and exchanging money while traveling. <u>It only verifies by voice call. Saily hasn't confirmed that Revolut's SMS 2FA works with the number.</u>
- **Klarna**: buy now, pay later, for splitting online purchases into installments.
- **Robinhood**, **Webull** and **eToro**: platforms for buying and selling stocks and other investments.
- **Gemini**: an exchange for buying and selling crypto.

Money apps make up most of that list, yet the same article says banks and payment apps are the likeliest to refuse the number. Both are true. The ones on the list have been reported working. For any bank or payment app that isn't on it, you'll have to try it yourself.

One more thing about PayPal: it plays a second role with Saily. When you buy the number or a data plan, you can pick PayPal at checkout ([payment methods](https://support.saily.com/hc/en-us/articles/31030534656924)). Paying Saily with PayPal and getting PayPal's codes on the number are two separate things.

## What users on X found: apps off the list

The official list is short, and a lot of what people care about, like Telegram, X and Google, isn't on it. Below are tests two users shared on X. They're those users' own experiences, not Saily's word, and the two didn't get the same results.

One user's roundup from October 2026 says that in a test on October 15, codes from Telegram, WhatsApp, X, TikTok, Discord, PayPal, Apple ID, Microsoft, Snapchat, Tinder and Amazon came through almost instantly. Facebook and Instagram moved the check over to WhatsApp. Google didn't ask for a phone number at sign-up, and a code arrived right away when the number was added later ([the post](https://x.com/yedabinbinah/status/2114683684414685509)).

Another user tested in September 2026. Codes from PayPal, X, Google and Apple arrived, but Telegram and WhatsApp each failed twice. They said they'd test again later ([the post](https://x.com/guyun_ai/status/2100200291539059164)).

Same app, one person gets the code instantly and another gets nothing. The number, the timing, or whether the phone let the text in can all make the difference. So a user's list tells you someone has made it work. It can't tell you it'll work for you.

In case some of these are new to you: **Telegram** is a chat app with lots of channels and groups. **X** is what used to be Twitter. **Discord** is chat rooms, popular with gaming and hobby communities. **TikTok** is short videos, **Snapchat** is disappearing messages and photos, and **Tinder** is dating. **Apple ID**, **Microsoft** and **Google** are those companies' accounts, and you'd usually add a number for password recovery and login checks. **Amazon** is online shopping.

## ChatGPT and Claude: the least predictable

Plenty of people want this number for AI services, and these are the least predictable. Neither is on the official list. The user who tested in September says OpenAI flatly refused to send a code to the Saily number, but sent one fine to a number from another carrier ([the post](https://x.com/guyun_ai/status/2100200291539059164)). The October roundup says support confirmed ChatGPT isn't guaranteed. After October 15 some numbers did get ChatGPT codes, but they took about 5 minutes. Don't tap "resend" while you wait, or it may be flagged as abuse and you'll wait about a day to try again. Claude isn't guaranteed either ([the post](https://x.com/yedabinbinah/status/2114683684414685509)).

If AI services are the main reason you want the number, go in knowing it might work and might not, and that today's result can change tomorrow.

## Once it's linked

Once the number is linked to PayPal, a bank or anything like that for 2FA, it's one of your keys to that account. So keep three things in mind.

1. **Don't let the number lapse.** If renewal stops, the number is held for only 30 more days, and after that it's gone for good. Changing the number in the app is the same: once the old one is swapped out, you can't get it back. Before you cancel or change it, update the phone number in each account first. See [Keep or change your number](/en/howto/keep-number/).
2. **Turn on another way in as well.** An authenticator app, a passkey or email. Saily notes these are often more secure than text codes anyway, and if something happens to the number, you still have a way in.
3. **When a code doesn't arrive, work out which side it is.** Either the app won't take this kind of number, or your phone didn't let the text in. [Texts and verification codes on the US number](/en/howto/us-number-sms/) walks through both.

<Callout type="tip">
Saily says that if a service won't accept the number, you can email support@saily.com and tell them which one. It helps them improve compatibility.
</Callout>

## How to check it worked

Start with an app where nothing much is at stake, like WhatsApp or Signal from the official list. If the code arrives, the number and your phone are both fine. Then link the services you actually need, one at a time. After each one, log out and log back in on the spot to make sure the code really comes through.

Keep that one line in mind: the lists are only a guide, and the only way to know whether your app takes the number is to try it. To keep the number yours for good, read [Keep or change your number](/en/howto/keep-number/) next.

## Stuck?

<Q q="I typed in the number and the app says it isn't supported.">
That app doesn't accept VoIP numbers, and trying again won't change it. See if it offers another way to verify (an authenticator, email or a passkey), or ask its support whether they can verify you differently. You can also tell Saily which service it was.
</Q>
<Q q="The app says it sent the code, but nothing shows up.">
Wait a few minutes and don't keep tapping resend. If there's another SIM in the phone, turn it off for now. More fixes are in [Texts and verification codes on the US number](/en/howto/us-number-sms/).
</Q>
<Q q="The app I want isn't on any list.">
Then the only way to know is to try. Test it with an account that doesn't matter first. If the code never comes, Saily lets you change the number once for free in the app, and not getting verification codes is one of the listed reasons. But once the old number is gone, it's gone, so read [Keep or change your number](/en/howto/keep-number/) before you change it.
</Q>
